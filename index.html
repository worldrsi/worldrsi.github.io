Abstract

Large multimodal models can now control robots as agents that read camera images and write code, with no task-specific training. Yet in a new scene such an agent sees only a few frames and gets a single attempt. A program written once from these frames breaks when the scene differs, and an agent that acts live discovers each mistake only after it has cost real steps.

We present WorldRSI, in which the agent rehearses in a world it builds before acting. It turns the first frames into a physics simulation, runs the task there and in perturbed copies, and writes what it learns into a harness, a playbook of steps, checks and repairs that a new agent follows on the real robot. On 640 held-out scenes of three robustness benchmarks built on LIBERO, WorldRSI raises Claude Opus 5.5 from 63.3% for a program written once and 67.8% for a live agent to 79.7%, and to 83.6% when rehearsal starts from a harness improved on earlier tasks, ahead of a fine-tuned π0.5 policy (72.7%). The gains hold for three agent models and carry over to bimanual and mobile manipulation and to two physical robots. On a Franka arm, WorldRSI succeeds in all 10 trials, against 5 for a live agent and 0 for a program written once.

On physical robots

Two robots that differ in arms, cameras and calibration. The Franka FR3 arm has a side and a wrist camera with only their intrinsics known, so the harness first calibrates the side camera from the gripper. The bimanual station has two 6-DoF arms and three RGB cameras without depth. On both, the objects are placed anew after the input frames, so the agent's world is never exact.

How it works

People prepare for a hard task in a safe place first. A surgeon rehearses a difficult operation, and a mover measures the staircase before carrying a sofa. What they take back is not a motion to replay but knowledge: what to check first, what can go wrong and what to do then.

WorldRSI gives a robot agent such a place, a simulation it builds from the first frames, and makes rehearsal produce knowledge rather than a policy. Only the harness reaches the real robot, where a new agent follows it while watching the cameras, so the world never has to be exact. At test time no part of WorldRSI sees the simulator's internal state or the benchmark's success signal.

  1. 1

    World Building

    The agent turns the first frames into a physics simulation of the objects that matter and the real robot, with a test that tells when the task is done.

  2. 2

    Rehearsal

    It runs the task in this world and in copies whose objects are shifted or moved during the run, and revises its plan after every failed dry run.

  3. 3

    Harness

    It writes what it learned into a playbook: the steps in order, a check after each, repairs for likely failures, a step budget and tested code.

  4. 4

    Live Execution

    A new agent follows the harness on the real robot, one short script at a time, and looks at the cameras after each to re-measure and repair.

Meta Harness

Because a harness holds knowledge rather than motions, it carries over to new tasks. A Meta Harness, improved beforehand on separate development tasks, first in worlds the agent built and then on development scenes, gives every rehearsal a better start (WorldRSI-Meta).

WorldRSI-Continuous

Along a sequence of tasks, each agent hands the reusable part of its harness to the next: skills, lessons, runnable plans and a recipe for building the world. Rehearsal then needs fewer tool calls from the second task on, and each task costs about 18% less.

Why rehearsal helps

A better world gives a better live run
When the agent places the objects of its world within 5 mm on average, the live stage succeeds in 91% of the episodes. Between 2 and 5 cm it succeeds in 39%.
Fewer surprises on the robot
A live agent without rehearsal most often runs out of steps. Rehearsal cuts these failures from 85 to 37, and grasp and placement failures from 47 to 28.
More reliable
Two runs of Online on the same scene disagree on a quarter of the scenes. Two runs of WorldRSI disagree on 13% and both succeed on 73%.
Less time on the robot
The live stage takes 2.5 minutes against 3.2 for Online, with fewer robot steps (222 against 248). On the Franka arm, WorldRSI reorders the cubes in 4.4 minutes against 8.2.

Worlds the agent built

Each world is built from the first frames of one scene. It only has to be good enough to rehearse in, because the live agent re-measures the real scene as its harness prescribes.
